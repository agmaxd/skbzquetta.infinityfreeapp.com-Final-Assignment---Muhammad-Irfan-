OUR STORY

A legacy built beyond borders.

The story of Shaikh Khalifa Bin Zayed Hospital is a story of partnership, perseverance, and a commitment to building a stronger future for healthcare in Balochistan.

MORE THAN A HOSPITAL

A story that began decades ago.

Shaikh Khalifa Bin Zayed Hospital in Quetta stands as part of a long-standing relationship between the United Arab Emirates and Pakistan.

Its story reaches back to the late 1990s, when the Government of the United Arab Emirates undertook the construction of the hospital as part of its support for healthcare and development in Pakistan.

What began as a large-scale healthcare project would eventually become part of the medical landscape of Quetta and Balochistan.

OUR HISTORY

From an idea to an institution.

Every institution has a beginning. Ours is a story that stretches across decades and borders.

0
THE BEGINNING

The hospital is completed.

In 1998, the Government of the United Arab Emirates completed the construction of what was known as the Sheikh Khalifa Bin Zayed Federal Hospital in Quetta.

The project represented a significant investment in healthcare infrastructure for the region and formed part of the wider relationship between the UAE and Pakistan.

0
AN AMBITIOUS VISION

Built for more than basic care.

Contemporary documentation described the facility as a 225-bed hospital designed to provide advanced healthcare services in areas including traumatology, cardiology and gastroenterology.

The vision extended beyond treating patients. The hospital was also intended to contribute to the training of nurses and medical technicians.

0
THE DOORS OPEN

From construction to care.

Building a hospital was only the beginning. Years of preparation followed before the facility began providing healthcare services.

According to records of the Senate of Pakistan, outpatient services began in April 2007, marking an important step in transforming the completed facility into a functioning healthcare institution.

---
A PARTNERSHIP ACROSS BORDERS

A connection between two nations.

The hospital became part of a broader relationship between the United Arab Emirates and Pakistan, where development and humanitarian assistance have supported communities across the country.

Healthcare became one of the important areas through which this relationship translated into tangible support for communities in need.

THE NAME BEHIND THE LEGACY

Sheikh Khalifa bin Zayed Al Nahyan.

The hospital carries the name of His Highness Sheikh Khalifa bin Zayed Al Nahyan, the late President of the United Arab Emirates and Ruler of Abu Dhabi.

Sheikh Khalifa's leadership was associated with humanitarian and development efforts extending beyond the borders of the UAE. Pakistan was among the countries to receive support through initiatives connected with his humanitarian vision.

0
CONTINUED SUPPORT

The commitment continued.

The story of the hospital did not end with its construction or opening.

In 2015, UAE foreign-aid records documented AED 27 million in support for medical equipment and maintenance at Sheikh Khalifa Hospital in Quetta.

Continued investment helped reinforce the idea that meaningful healthcare infrastructure requires long-term commitment, not simply a building.

A LEGACY OF HUMANITARIANISM

A name that carries a wider story.

Sheikh Khalifa bin Zayed Al Nahyan's legacy was not defined only by leadership within the United Arab Emirates. His name became associated with humanitarian and development initiatives reaching communities beyond the UAE.

In Pakistan, that legacy included support for healthcare, education, infrastructure, and communities affected by natural disasters.

The hospital in Quetta stands as one part of that wider story — a physical reminder of how humanitarian partnerships can cross borders and endure through generations.

THE JOURNEY CONTINUES

Built decades ago. Still looking forward.

Healthcare is never a finished project. Hospitals evolve with the communities they serve, with advances in medicine, technology, and the changing needs of patients.

From its foundations in 1998 to its continuing role in Quetta, the story of Shaikh Khalifa Bin Zayed Hospital is ultimately a story about people.

People who built it. People who supported it. Professionals who work within it. And, most importantly, the patients and families whose lives give the institution its purpose.

A hospital may begin with bricks, steel, and equipment — but its true legacy is built through the lives it touches.
